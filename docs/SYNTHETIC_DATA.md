# Synthetic Data

This repository runs on generated data. The pipeline was developed against a
real hotel's property management system extract, which cannot be published and
whose source property is not identified.

Rather than ship an empty repository or a handful of toy rows, the generator
encodes an explicit model of hotel demand. It is worth reading on its own terms:
`src/data/generate_synthetic_data.py`.

---

## What is fictional

Everything identifying. The property in `config/config.yaml` — its name,
location, room mix, capacity and event calendar — describes a hotel that does
not exist. Room type names, the number of each, and the local event calendar are
all invented.

## What is preserved

The statistical behaviour the pipeline was built to handle. Specifically:

**Seasonality.** Monthly demand multipliers with soft deep-winter months and
strong shoulder-season and summer peaks. Weekday multipliers with midweek
business strength and a weak Sunday.

**Booking curves.** The fraction of final demand on the books at each lead time.
Roughly 13% at 90 days, 23% at 60, 40% at 30, rising to 100% at arrival — about
half of all bookings landing in the final month, which is typical for a city
hotel with a mixed leisure and corporate base.

**Demand dispersion by room type.** Coefficient of variation ranging from around
0.27 on high-volume types to 0.70 on the smallest — the spread that makes
uniform modelling across types inappropriate.

**Reservation lifecycles.** Cancellation and no-show rates applied at the
reservation level, so realised demand is genuinely net of attrition rather than
assumed clean.

**Event-driven spikes.** Ordinal impact levels translating to demand
multipliers, applied on the relevant dates.

**Year-over-year regime shifts.** Annual demand multipliers that vary
meaningfully between years. This is what makes naive same-period-last-year
forecasting fragile and pace-aware features necessary — without it, the dataset
would be easier than reality.

**Right-skewed noise.** Demand is drawn lognormally rather than normally, so it
stays positive and carries the upward tail that real occupancy distributions
show.

## Two artefacts

**`data/processed/pickup_features.csv`** — the engineered feature table used for
training. One row per arrival date and room type, with realised demand and
reconstructed book positions at each lead time.

**`data/raw/pms_exports/*.csv`** — a headerless, row-per-room-per-night export
mimicking the PMS report the inference pipeline consumes, including forward
reservations, cancellations and no-shows. This is what the app and CLI read.

The forward export respects the booking curve: for a stay date 60 days out, only
the share of demand that would realistically have booked by the snapshot date
exists. An earlier version generated all bookings regardless of lead time, which
handed the model a book position no real export would ever show and produced
forecasts above 100% occupancy across the board.

## Regenerating

```bash
python src/data/generate_synthetic_data.py
python src/data/generate_synthetic_data.py --seed 7
python src/data/generate_synthetic_data.py --start 2021-01-01 --end 2025-12-31
```

Default seed is 42, so results are reproducible. Changing the seed changes the
data and therefore the reported metrics — the improvement over baseline is
stable across seeds, the exact figures are not.

## What this does and does not demonstrate

**Does:** the pipeline architecture, feature engineering, validation
methodology, the diagnostic work behind the monotonic constraints, and the
interface. All of this is real and reproducible.

**Does not:** that the model achieves any particular accuracy on the original
property. Generated data is, unavoidably, generated by a process the model is
well suited to learn. The headline improvement over baseline here should be read
as a demonstration that the evaluation harness works, not as a performance claim
about the real deployment.

The design decisions came from the real deployment. The numbers in this
repository come from synthetic data. Both are stated plainly rather than blurred
together.
